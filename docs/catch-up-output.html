What the Catch-up prompt gives you

After you paste prompt.md as your first message, the chatbot asks a few short setup questions. It then writes a Catch-up: a spoiler-free account of the book up to the last chapter you finished. Once you've read it, you continue from the next chapter. The examples on this page use Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde, read through chapter 7.

Setup

One question per message: the book, where you stopped, how much detail, which language.

Catch-up

One Markdown document: the story so far, where you are, open threads, then reference.

Export

A .md download if the chatbot can create files. Otherwise, instructions for saving it.

Follow-ups

Questions about what you've read. Nothing past where you stopped.

1 · The setup conversation

The chatbot asks one question at a time and skips anything you've already told it. If you can't upload the book, it offers an online library search (when the chatbot can search) or pasting the text. It never works from its own memory of the book.

ChatbotHi! I'll write a spoiler-free catch-up of your book up to the chapter you finished. First, please upload the book. EPUB or a text-based PDF works best here.

2 · Anatomy of a Catch-up

Every Catch-up has this shape. On the left is an excerpt of what the chatbot writes. On the right is why each part is there.

# heading + > header block
Catch-up: Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde by Robert Louis Stevenson
Edition: English · Book Text: uploaded · Created: 2026-11-18
Covers: from the start through the end of Chapter 7. No spoilers past this point. Continue reading with the next chapter.

Header

Says which book and which Edition were used, where the text came from, and exactly where the Catch-up stops. It deliberately doesn't name the next chapter, because chapter titles can give away plot.

The story so far

The will. The lawyer Utterson has an old friend, Dr Jekyll, and a growing worry about him. On a Sunday walk, Utterson's relative Enfield tells of a small man who trampled a child at night, then paid her family with a cheque signed by a highly respectable name (Ch. 1). The man's name, Hyde, alarms Utterson, because Jekyll's will leaves everything to an Edward Hyde…

The murder. … The withdrawal. …

The story so far

Connected prose grouped by story arc, not one bullet per chapter. Events are linked by cause and goal ("because", "so"), since that chain is what memory keeps. Names are bold at first mention, and "(Ch. N)" anchors help you find events. About 500 words in Quick, 1,000 in Detailed; every other section scales with it.

Where you are

Hyde has vanished since the murder, Lanyon is dead, and Jekyll has shut himself away, ill. Utterson still wants to understand Jekyll's tie to Hyde.

In the last scene, Utterson and Enfield step into the courtyard and see Jekyll at a half-open window, "like some disconsolate prisoner". Mid-conversation, his smile is struck from his face by "an expression of such abject terror and despair, as froze the very blood" of the men below. The window is thrust down…

Where you are

Where things stand now, then the last scene up close, ending exactly where you stopped. One or two short quotes from the book act as memory cues, because the book's own words trigger recall better than a paraphrase.

Open threads

  • What hold does Hyde have over Jekyll, and why does the will provide for a mere "disappearance"?
  • What shock destroyed Lanyon, and what is in his envelope?
  • Utterson still means to learn what Jekyll is hiding. Jekyll means to stay shut away.

Continue with the next chapter.

Open threads

Unanswered questions and characters' unfinished intentions, exactly as open as the book leaves them. They're the last thing you read, so they're fresh in your mind when you open the next chapter.

--- · Reference: look up as needed.

Characters so far

  • Utterson: reserved, loyal lawyer. Wants to protect Jekyll and understand his tie to Hyde.
  • Dr Henry Jekyll: respected doctor, Utterson's friend. Wants to be left alone; says he is finished with Hyde.
  • Edward Hyde: small man who disgusts everyone who meets him. Jekyll's heir, Carew's murderer, now vanished.

World and terms

  • The cabinet: Jekyll's private room above the laboratory, where he now shuts himself away.
  • The shabby door: the laboratory's rear entrance on a back street, where Enfield saw Hyde go in.

Reference

Below a divider, so it stays out of the reading flow, like Kindle X-Ray. Characters list what each one wants now and what you know about them, ordered by how much the book has covered them so far, never by later importance. World and terms uses the same format. It's left out if the book has nothing to list.

3 · What it will never write

These spoiler rules override everything else, including what you ask for.

Only the text you've read

The model ignores what it already knows about the book, even for a classic whose twist everyone knows.
Never: "Hyde, Jekyll's darker self…"
Instead: "Hyde, a man Jekyll protects for reasons he won't explain."

No hints about what comes later

Never: "Lanyon's envelope will prove crucial…"
Instead: "Lanyon leaves Utterson a sealed envelope."

No early answers

Mysteries stay exactly as open as they are at your chapter.
Never: "Jekyll forged the letter himself."
Instead: "Utterson suspects Jekyll forged the letter to shield Hyde."

No later chapter titles

Not in the header, not in setup, not anywhere.
Never: "Next up: [title of chapter 8]"
Instead: "Continue reading with the next chapter."

4 · Getting the file

The prompt checks what the chatbot can do and only asks you to do the steps it can't.

5 · After the Catch-up

You can keep asking questions. The chatbot answers from the chapters you've read. For everything else it gives one fixed reply, the same whether your guess is right, wrong or about something that never happens.

YouRemind me who Poole is?
ChatbotPoole is Jekyll's longtime butler. In chapter 2 he tells Utterson that the servants have orders to obey Hyde, who has a key to the laboratory.
YouIs Hyde secretly Jekyll's son?
ChatbotThat isn't covered in what you've read so far.
YouI've just finished chapter 8 too, update it?
ChatbotThis Catch-up stays at chapter 7. For a new stopping point, start a new chat with the prompt.
